<h2>II. What's the difference between spot and futures? Beginners should understand first</h2>

<p>Many beginners transition from spot to futures, but the risk structures of the two are completely different.</p>

<table border="1" cellpadding="8" cellspacing="0">
<tr><th>Comparison Item</th><th>Spot Trading</th><th>USDT-M Perpetual Futures</th></tr>
<tr><td>Held Asset</td><td>Actually buy coins</td><td>Trade contracts, don't hold coins</td></tr>
<tr><td>Leverage</td><td>None</td><td>From 1x, some pairs up to 125x</td></tr>
<tr><td>Direction</td><td>Only long (buy first, sell later)</td><td>Can go long or short</td></tr>
<tr><td>Maximum Loss</td><td>Invested principal (coin price goes to zero)</td><td>May be force-liquidated, losing margin</td></tr>
<tr><td>Fees</td><td>Spot Maker / Taker</td><td>Futures Maker / Taker, plus funding rate</td></tr>
<tr><td>Suitable for Beginners</td><td>Start here</td><td>Enter after understanding leverage and liquidation</td></tr>
</table>

<p>1. What futures profit from is "direction." Both long and short are possible. Correct judgment yields profit; wrong direction means loss. Leverage amplifies both sides simultaneously.</p>

<p>2. Funding rate is a cost unique to futures. Perpetual contracts have no expiry date. The funding rate, settled every 8 hours, keeps the contract price close to spot. The rate can be positive or negative. For long-term positions, factor it into your costs.</p>

<p>3. The liquidation mechanism is the biggest difference. Spot can be held indefinitely waiting for a recovery if the price drops; futures is margin trading — when the loss reaches the maintenance margin line, the system force-liquidates without needing your consent.</p>

<h2>V. How to choose leverage? Cross vs Isolated</h2>

<p><strong>Leverage Multiplier</strong><br>
Some pairs can go up to 125x, but beginners are not advised to exceed 5x. The higher the leverage, the closer the liquidation price is to the opening price — a single fluctuation can knock you out.</p>

<table border="1" cellpadding="8" cellspacing="0">
<tr><th>Leverage</th><th>Suitable For</th><th>Characteristics</th></tr>
<tr><td>1–3x</td><td>Complete beginners</td><td>Liquidation distance is far, large margin for error</td></tr>
<tr><td>Around 5x</td><td>Experienced</td><td>Common swing trading setting</td></tr>
<tr><td>Above 10x</td><td>Experienced traders</td><td>Requires strict stop-loss</td></tr>
<tr><td>Above 50x</td><td>Scalping veterans</td><td>Extremely easy to get liquidated by wicks</td></tr>
</table>

<p><strong>Cross vs Isolated</strong><br>
<strong>Cross:</strong> The entire futures account balance serves as margin. A loss in one position is borne by the whole account, offering strong resistance to wicks, but extreme market conditions may force-liquidate other positions together.<br>
<strong>Isolated:</strong> Each position only uses the allocated margin. The maximum loss is that position's margin, without affecting other funds in the account.</p>

<p>💡 Beginner suggestion: Isolated + Low leverage + Small position. Learn to control the maximum loss per trade before considering scaling up.</p>

<h2>VI. ⚠️ How is liquidation price calculated? Three major futures risks</h2>

<p><strong>Liquidation</strong><br>
When account equity falls below the maintenance margin requirement, the system force-liquidates at the bankruptcy price. Factors affecting liquidation price: leverage multiplier, opening price, position size, and maintenance margin rate.</p>

<p>Rough concept: Under isolated mode, liquidation triggers when the loss reaches a certain percentage of the margin (depending on the maintenance margin rate). The higher the leverage, the smaller the reverse fluctuation you can withstand — at 10x leverage, a reverse fluctuation of a few percentage points may trigger liquidation.</p>

<p><strong>Mark Price vs Last Price</strong><br>
Liquidation judgment uses the Mark Price (weighted index of multiple exchanges), not a single platform's last price. This reduces the chance of a single-exchange wick mistakenly hitting your position. But this doesn't mean you won't be liquidated — it's just relatively fair.</p>

<h2>IX. 📈 How to set TP/SL?</h2>

<p>It's recommended to set Take Profit / Stop Loss (TP/SL) together when placing the order, not after opening the position.</p>

<p><strong>Setting Location:</strong><br>
Futures order panel → Advanced settings / TP & SL → Enter trigger price → Submit together.</p>

<p><strong>Common Approaches:</strong><br>
Fixed ratio: For example, set stop-loss at 2–3% reverse from the opening price (converted to loss based on actual leverage and margin ratio), and take profit at 2–3 times the stop-loss distance forward.<br>
Technical levels: Place stop-loss outside key support/resistance to avoid being swept exactly.<br>
Reduce-Only: Ensures the order only reduces the position, never adds reversely.</p>

<p><strong>Three Principles:</strong><br>
Stop-loss is for survival, prioritized over take profit.<br>
Stop-loss distance must match leverage: the higher the leverage, the closer the stop-loss must be.<br>
Before opening, decide "what's the maximum I can lose on this trade," then determine position size.</p>
