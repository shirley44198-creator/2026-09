<h2>7. 🛡️ Risk Control: Liquidation Price, Funding Rate and Stop Loss</h2>
<p><strong>What is liquidation price?</strong><br>
Liquidation price is the price at which adverse market movement leaves margin insufficient to maintain the position. The higher the leverage, the closer the liquidation price is to the entry price. The order confirmation page shows the "estimated liquidation price" — always check it before opening.</p>

<p><strong>Four risk control principles for beginners</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Keep each position's risk within 5–10% of total capital</li>
<li>Start with 2–3x leverage; don't raise it just because of one or two wins</li>
<li>Set a stop loss on every order; decide the stop level before opening</li>
<li>Don't hold losing positions or add dead money: when the direction is wrong, averaging down often accelerates losses</li>
</ol>
<p>⚠️ In futures markets, surviving matters more than making money. Liquidation is instant; recovery is long-term. Any message claiming "guaranteed profit" or "signal-based sure wins" is a scam — don't trust so-called "teachers" who DM you.</p>

Perpetual futures have no expiry, so the "funding rate" anchors the futures price to the spot price. Longs and shorts pay each other periodically; holding through the settlement time incurs a fee or yields a gain.</p>

<p><strong>How do I save on futures fees?</strong><br>
Three ways: register with <strong>BTC9149</strong> for a 20% rebate, use more limit orders (Maker), and avoid extreme funding rate periods.</p>

In Isolated mode you at most lose that position's margin; in Cross mode more of the account balance may be used. This is also why beginners should use Isolated.</p>

<p><strong>Can I short?</strong><br>
Yes. Futures support long (buy to open long) and short (sell to open short) — one of the core differences between futures and spot.</p>
