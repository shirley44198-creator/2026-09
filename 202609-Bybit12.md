<h2>IV. Method 2: Limit Order — Specify Your Price</h2>

<p>A limit order means "buy only when the price reaches my target." You set a target price; the order executes only when the market drops to that level. If it doesn't reach, no purchase happens and funds stay in your account.</p>

<p>Process (4 steps):</p>

<p>1. Enter the trading pair, switch to "Limit."<br>
2. Enter the limit price (e.g., if you want to buy BTC at 60,000 USDT, enter 60,000).<br>
3. Enter the purchase quantity, confirm the total order amount.<br>
4. Click "Buy BTC" — the order enters the order book, waiting for the market to hit your price.</p>

<p>Advantages: ✅ Cost is controllable, you decide the price ✅ Limit orders are usually Maker, with lower fees or even discounts ✅ Suitable for those with a target price</p>

<p>Notes: ⚠️ No guarantee of execution; if the price never drops, you won't buy ⚠️ Requires understanding the "pending order" concept ⚠️ Easy to miss out in a rally</p>

<h2>V. Method 3: Conditional Order — Auto Stop-Loss / Take-Profit</h2>

<p>A conditional order means "set a trigger condition first, and it executes automatically when triggered." The most common use is stop-loss: when holding BTC, set "auto sell at market price if it drops below a certain level," so you don't have to watch the market all day.</p>

<p>Process (4 steps):</p>

<p>1. Enter the trading pair, switch to "Conditional."<br>
2. Set the trigger condition (e.g., BTC mark price drops below 58,000).<br>
3. Set the order type after triggering (Market or Limit).<br>
4. Confirm and submit. When the condition is met, the system automatically executes the sell.</p>

<p>Advantages: ✅ Control risk without watching the market ✅ Suitable for holding overnight ✅ Can set both stop-loss and take-profit</p>

<p>Notes: ⚠️ Extreme market conditions (sharp drops, wicks) may result in execution at a worse price ⚠️ Trigger price ≠ guaranteed execution price ⚠️ Conditional orders are not canceled by closing the App; you must manage unfilled orders yourself</p>
