<h2>VI. Three Common Withdrawal Scams</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Fake payment screenshot:</strong> The other party sends a "transfer success" screenshot to rush you into releasing. Trust only your own banking app.</li>
  <li><strong>Off-platform high-price buyer:</strong> Private messages offering "above-market price for your USDT," luring you off-platform — you may lose both money and coins.</li>
  <li><strong>Fake customer support:</strong> Someone claiming to be Binance support says your account is abnormal and asks you to "verify assets" or provide a code. Binance will never DM you asking for transfers or SMS codes.</li>
</ul>

<h2>VII. When can't you cancel or release?</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Buyer paid & you confirmed receipt:</strong> Release promptly; unjustified delay may lead to reports that hurt your account credit.</li>
  <li><strong>Order in progress, buyer hasn't paid:</strong> Follow the normal order flow, but don't repeatedly create and cancel orders just to "find a higher-priced buyer."</li>
  <li><strong>Doubts about the fund source:</strong> Don't release or refund. Use the order appeal function and let the platform step in.</li>
</ul>

<h2>VIII. How to reduce bank-account risk when withdrawing?</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Prioritize buyers with a stable historical record — trade volume and completion rate matter more than a slightly higher buy price.</li>
  <li>Use your own bank account to receive; the simpler the payer/recipient info, the better.</li>
  <li>Don't trade outside the platform — leaving the C2C system voids order protection and the evidence chain.</li>
  <li>Keep withdrawal records: order number, credit proof, and relevant chat history.</li>
  <li>Follow bank and regional rules. Different banks have very different attitudes toward crypto-related inflows/outflows — don't use false purposes or split orders to bypass bank controls.</li>
</ul>
<p class="warning"><strong>So-called "anti-freeze-card tricks" ≠ bypassing bank controls.</strong> The truly workable approach: choose trustworthy buyers, receive to your own account, keep records, and avoid funds of unknown origin.</p>
